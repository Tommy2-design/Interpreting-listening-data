Interpreting Social Media Listening Data

Listening goes beyond your own posts. Understand what the broader public is saying — about your brand, your industry, and your competitors.

Background — What Is Social Listening?

Monitoring vs. Listening — The Key Difference

While monitoring tracks what the brand posts, listening captures what the public says — including people who have never followed or interacted with the brand. Listening draws from public conversations across social media, news sites, blogs, forums, and review platforms.


Listening answers the question: What do people think and feel about this brand, category, or topic — and why?

Customer Insights

What do sentiment patterns and keyword associations reveal about how customers perceive the brand?

Industry Insights

What broader trends are shaping the category conversation? Is the brand aligned or missing a key topic?

Competitor Insights

How does the brand's share of voice compare? What topics does each competitor dominate online?

Key Listening Metrics

What Listening Tools Give You

  • Mention Volume — total times the brand or keyword appears in public content. Must be contextualised by time period and industry scale.
  • Mentions Over Time — a timeline showing spikes. Spikes often correspond to campaigns, news events, or public incidents.
  • Sentiment Split — the proportion of positive, neutral, and negative mentions. Use AI-generated sentiment as a starting point — always manually verify samples.
  • Share of Voice (SoV) — your brand's proportion of total category mentions versus competitors. Reflects conversation dominance, not market share.
  • Keyword/Topic Clusters — recurring themes and associations in the conversation around your brand or category.
Important: AI-powered sentiment tools frequently misclassify sarcasm, mixed emotions, and language nuance. Always review a sample of raw mentions before drawing conclusions from a sentiment score.

Customer Insight — Sentiment Interpretation

Using the sentiment data and keyword associations, write a 4–5 sentence customer insight paragraph. Identify one positive theme and one negative theme. Explain what each reveals about how customers perceive Brand Lumio — and support your analysis with an academic or industry source. What does this mean for the brand?

Model Answer

Brand Lumio's listening data reveals a broadly favourable customer sentiment, with 58% of mentions classified as positive over the four-week window. The clustering of keywords such as "thiên nhiên" (natural) and "dưỡng ẩm" (moisturising) suggests that the brand's clean ingredient positioning is resonating with its audience — consistent with research indicating that perceived naturalness is a primary driver of purchase intention and loyalty in the Vietnamese personal care category (Nielsen Vietnam, 2024). However, a recurring negative theme — the price-to-size ratio — reveals a perception gap between the brand's premium packaging aspiration and its audience's value expectations. This tension indicates that Lumio may need to either revise its packaging sizing strategy or strengthen its communication of premium ingredient quality to justify the price point and reduce the risk of negative word-of-mouth amplified through social channels.

2
Industry Insight — Trend Identification

Using the Industry Insights tab data, write a 4–5 sentence industry insight paragraph. Identify one significant trend from the category conversation. Explain whether Brand Lumio is currently positioned to benefit from or is missing this trend — and what the brand should consider. Cite a source.

Model Answer

Category listening data for "skincare Việt" reveals a 34% month-on-month increase in total mentions, with "clean beauty" and natural ingredient transparency emerging as the dominant conversation themes. This trend indicates a structural shift in Vietnamese consumer values toward ingredient literacy and brand accountability — consistent with Decision Lab's (2024) findings that Gen Z consumers in Vietnam are increasingly scrutinising formulation claims before purchase. While Brand Lumio's current keyword associations suggest it is partially aligned with this trend through its "thiên nhiên" positioning, the brand is absent from the emerging "sustainable packaging" conversation — a topic that represents 9% of category mentions but zero share of Lumio's content output. This gap suggests that proactively communicating packaging sustainability commitments could both strengthen brand legitimacy and pre-empt a potential reputational risk as consumer expectations escalate across the category.

3
Competitor Insight — Share of Voice Analysis

Using the Competitor Insights tab, write a 4–5 sentence competitor insight paragraph. Compare Brand Lumio's Share of Voice to Competitor A (Noni). What does the data reveal about each brand's positioning in the online conversation? What is the strategic implication for Lumio? Cite a source.

Model Answer

Share of Voice data indicates that Brand Lumio currently leads the Vietnamese clean skincare category with 42% of total mentions, compared to Competitor A (Noni) at 31% — giving Lumio a meaningful conversation advantage in the short term. However, Noni's 22% month-on-month growth rate and its dominance of the "skin barrier" and "dermatologist" keyword clusters reveals a strategically significant positioning gap: while Lumio leads on volume, Noni is winning on the credibility-linked language that drives conversion in higher-involvement product categories (Tuten & Solomon, 2023). This pattern suggests that share of voice volume alone is insufficient as a performance indicator — the thematic content of those mentions matters equally, as brands that own expert-aligned keywords tend to attract higher-trust consumer segments. For Brand Lumio, the strategic implication is clear: without investment in clinical validation or expert-endorsed content, Noni's momentum may convert into a market share advantage among the 20–29 consumer segment where decision-making is most influenced by peer and authority signals.
